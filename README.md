I am a Master's student in Classical Philology at the University of Naples Federico II and Scuola Superiore Meridionale, specializing in the intersection between traditional textual criticism and digital instruments.

My primary research interest lies in applying computational methods, such as Handwritten Text Recognition (HTR), Automatic Text Recognition (ATR) to ancient Greek and Latin manuscripts, mastering my competences in platforms such as Escriptorium and Trasnkribus.

This repository serves as a personal sandbox documenting my self-taught journey into Digital Humanities and Data Science, field that I am approaching through IBM Data science course. It contains my first practical experiments in structuring, analyzing, and transcribing historical text data. 
This is the structure:

- ATR experiments: Tests in Layout Analysis and Ground Truth (GT) preparation using Transkribus and Escriptorium. On Transkribus, I selected a sample manuscript from the Homer Multitext Project, applied a public recognition model (Byzantine Greek), and manually corrected the ATR output. ON Escriptorium, I segmented the text, recognized lines, transcribed after confronting the text offered by the model PP-OCRv6 (medium) multilingual handwritten and printed text recognition model [Kiessling, 2026. 10.5281/zenodo.21788410] and the model [Latin Script Medieval] CATMuS Medieval 1.6.0, Pinche et al. (10.5281/zenodo.15030337). 

- XML/TEI Encoding: Basic semantic markup experiments using the TEI (Text Encoding Initiatives) guidelines. I’d like to encode structural elements, abbreviations, and textual variants in Latin/Greek snippets.

- Python Scripts (Work in progress) Basic scripts for text manipulation, tokenization, and word frequency analysis applied to .txt exports of ancient texts. Applying the foundational concepts I am currently learning through the IBM Data Science Professional.
